Orientation Week is Dalhousie's largest student-facing event of the year. Thousands of incoming students across every faculty arrive on campus for the first time, and the week is designed to help them find their footing.

## My role

As Campus Engagement Assistant at Student Affairs, I had full ownership of Orientation Week for 2025. That meant building the promotional strategy from scratch, coordinating logistics across multiple teams, and being on-site for the entire week to manage execution.

## The promotional strategy

We ran campaigns across three channels: email to incoming students, Instagram, and physical signage across campus. The goal was not just reach but coherence. Every touchpoint needed to feel like it came from the same place and point toward the same thing: showing up.

The hardest part was writing copy that felt warm and specific without being performative. Incoming students can tell when institutions are trying too hard to seem welcoming. The tone we settled on was more like a letter from someone who actually goes here.

## What I learned

Running an event at this scale is mostly a logistics problem. But the interesting problems are the ones you cannot put in a spreadsheet. What do you do when the schedule shifts and 200 students are waiting with nowhere to go? How do you make a student who showed up alone feel like they made the right call?

The answer to both is the same: you design for the person, not the event. You think about what it feels like to be the person walking in, not what it looks like from the stage.
