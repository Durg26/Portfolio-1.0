Event Tree started from a frustration that I suspect many students share. There were things happening on campus every week. Most students did not know about them until it was too late, if at all.

The information existed. It just did not travel.

## The problem

Campus communication is fragmented by design. Each faculty, society, department, and team communicates independently, through their own channels, with their own audiences. A student following fifty accounts still misses most of what is happening.

Event Tree was built to be the layer on top of all of that. One place where students could see what was happening across campus, organized in a way that made sense for how students actually use their phones.

## Building it

I handled the concept, branding, UX design, and promotional strategy independently. The technical side required learning as I went. The harder part was the design problem: how do you make a discovery platform feel warm and human rather than like a search engine?

The answer I came to was curation. Not just aggregation but selection. Highlighting the events that deserved more attention than their organizers' Instagram following could give them.

## What it means to me

Event Tree is the project I am most proud of, not because it is the most polished thing I have built, but because it started with a genuine problem and became a genuine attempt to solve it.

Whether or not it scales, the impulse behind it is the one I want to keep having: see a gap, fill it, do not wait for permission.
