For two semesters as Marketing and PR Director at the Dalhousie Science Society, I led campaigns for more than four events per semester. The events ranged from casual social mixers to academic panels to the Society's annual formal.

## The challenge

Student society events live and die by turnout. The students you want to reach are busy, bombarded with notifications, and mostly indifferent to generic "come join us" messaging.

The societies that break through are the ones that make specific promises. Not "a great evening" but "this particular thing, at this particular time, designed for someone like you."

## What we built

We redesigned the promotional approach from the ground up. Each event got a distinct visual identity that connected to the Society's overall brand but felt tailored to that event. Copy was written for the person most likely to actually come, not for the broadest possible audience.

We also started using Instagram Stories more aggressively in the 48 hours before each event. A single well-timed story with the right energy could move real numbers.

## Results

Over the two semesters, we consistently hit and exceeded turnout targets. More importantly, the events started to feel different. Students mentioned them to each other not just because they saw a post but because they expected them to be worth going to.

That shift in expectation is harder to build than any single campaign.
